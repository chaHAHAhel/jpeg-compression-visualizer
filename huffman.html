Grayscale Conversion

💡 Why Grayscale?

A full color image has over 16 million possible RGB colors! By converting to grayscale, we limit our "alphabet" to just 256 possible shades of gray. This makes it perfect for visualizing a Huffman Tree.

3 The Huffman Dictionary

The algorithm built a binary tree where the most frequent colors are at the top (short codes) and rare colors are at the bottom (long codes). Here is the final mapping.

🧠 Variable-Length Magic

Standard raw images use exactly 8 bits for every single pixel, regardless of how common it is. Notice how the most common colors now use 2, 3, or 4 bits! However, very rare colors might end up using 10 or 12 bits. As long as the common colors heavily outnumber the rare ones, we save massive amounts of space overall.

4 Compression Result

🤔 Why isn't the file 10x smaller?

Raw pixel data in photographs is very "noisy", meaning almost all 256 shades of gray are used relatively equally. Huffman coding works best when there's a massive imbalance in frequencies. This is exactly why JPEG first uses DCT and Quantization — to mathematically force the data into long sequences of zeros (creating a massive imbalance) before finally applying Huffman coding to shrink it down to a tiny size!
